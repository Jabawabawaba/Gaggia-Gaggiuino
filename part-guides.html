Thermocouple

K type · M4 × 6 mm

You need
  • The thermocouple: K type, ungrounded, M4 thread
  • Thermal paste rated for 180 °C or more
  • A spanner for the old thermostat
  • Hex keys for the boiler's four screws, if you tip it up
  • A multimeter
What it does

It reports the boiler's temperature to the PCB on J4. Ungrounded means its junction is insulated from the probe body, because the low-voltage side floats; How Gaggiuino works explains why.

    Pressure sensor

    1.2 MPa · 0 to 12 bar

    You need
    • The sensor and its O-rings
    • The tap block with its small push-in fitting
    • The sensor's push-in fitting (1/4" thread to 6 mm tube)
    • 40 mm of 4 × 6 mm PTFE tube and a sharp cutter
    • Two longer M5 screws (25 mm on the official parts list)
    • A towel
    • The sensor and its flat O-ring
    • Classic Pro family: a 6 mm barbed T, a 6 mm barb to 1/4" sensor fitting, 200 to 250 mm of braided hose, four hose clamps and clamp pliers
    • Original Classic: a 6 mm push-to-connect T, a push-in sensor fitting (1/4" to 6 mm), 200 to 250 mm of 4 × 6 mm PTFE tube
    • A sharp cutter
    What it does

    It tells the PCB the brew pressure, 0 to 12 bar, on J6, so Gaggiuino can profile pressure. It seals on an O-ring, not on its thread.

    Two O-ring jobs. A flat white one, about 11 mm across and 3 mm thick, seals the sensor in its fitting. A small round silicone one, 6 mm inside and 1.8 mm thick, seals the block against the group. Check your kit has one of each: the block's is a size 010 O-ring.

      Resettable fuse

      185 °C · 250 V 16 A · optional

      Optional. The official wiring basics page says a kit's resettable fuse goes in the steam thermostat's slot as a replacement for the stock 184 °C one-shot fuse, on any install. The stock wiring guide itself keeps the stock fuse and gives no steps for the swap, so these follow the custom wiring guide: the old fuse's wires move onto the new one. If you'd rather follow the stock guide exactly, keep it as a spare.

      On stock wiring it carries everything the machine draws: about 12 A at 120 V and 6 A at 230 V, inside its 16 A rating.

      Optional. The custom wiring guide lists it as a substitute for the stock one-shot fuse, in the steam thermostat's hole. In custom wiring it sits in the heater line.

      You need
      • The resettable fuse: 185 °C, 16 A, M4 stud
      • The same 180 °C thermal paste
      • Uninsulated 6.3 mm female spades, silicone sleeve and an open-barrel crimper
      • 18 AWG silicone wire, a bare BN1.25 butt splice and silicone heat shrink, if a wire won't reach
      • A multimeter
      What it does

      Like the thermostats it's a bimetal switch, but it opens only at 185 °C, as a last resort if the boiler overheats, and stays open until you press its button. The stock fuse does the same job once and then has to be replaced.

        Making the wires

        strip · crimp · insulate · pull-test

        You need
        • 18 AWG silicone wire for mains, 22 AWG for low voltage, cut from the cut list
        • A wire stripper
        • A ratcheting crimper for insulated terminals (red, blue, yellow dies)
        • An open-barrel crimper for the bare spades at the boiler, and an uninsulated-terminal crimper for any BN splices
        • 6.3 mm spades (insulated, bare and piggyback), forks, BN1.25 splices
        • Silicone heat shrink or self-fusing silicone tape, and a heat gun
        Which crimper is which

        An insulated-terminal crimper has colored, rounded dies and squashes a nylon sleeve. An open-barrel crimper has small B-shaped dies that roll a bare terminal's tabs over the wire. A third kind, with a punch and block, crimps bare BN splices. Most hardware-store crimpers are the insulated kind; bare open-barrel spades at the boiler need the open-barrel kind.

          Solid-state relay

          SSR-40DA · back wall

          You need
          • The SSR (SSR-40DA)
          • An M4 × 10 screw, washer and nut, or an 8-32 set
          • Forks for all four screws: SV1.25-5 for one wire, SV2-5 for two, as on the official parts list
          • A screwdriver for the terminal screws
          What it does

          It switches the heater on the board's command, with the board's 5 V side isolated from mains. How Gaggiuino works explains it.

            Switches

            stock wiring

            On custom wiring the brew and steam switches carry only the low-voltage wires your harness drawing shows. The moves below are for stock wiring.

            You need
            • Your printed wire labels
            • Silicone tape or silicone heat shrink for the connectors that stay live
            • Cable ties
            • The low-voltage switch wires, with 6.3 mm female spades (the ground and its jumper share one)
            What changes

            The switches also become signals for the PCB: one pole of each, when on, joins a PCB input to the 5 V ground. The other spades still carry mains, so the low-voltage wires go only on the ones this frees, and the moved connectors stay live.
