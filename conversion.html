01

The stock Gaggia

A small aluminumlead-free brass boiler, a vibratory pump and a thermostat that switches the heater fully on and fully off. The temperature swings above and below the target, which is why owners learned to time their shots by the brew lampready lamp.

How the Gaggia works
02

Plan and buy

An approved supplier's kit has the PCB, screen, sensors and SSR, but no mains wiring. Add 18 AWG silicone wire and crimps, printed housings and a multimeter.

BudgetUsed Gaggia checkParts tracker
03

Open it up

Cord out of the wall. Take out two screws, lift off the cup warmer and unplug its earth wires. Photograph and label every stock wire, then take the brew thermostat out of the side of the boiler.

RunbookModel notes
04

Fit the hardware

The thermocouple screws into the brew thermostat's hole. The SSR goes on the back wall, the PCB in its housing on the right, the pressure sensor on a tap block or a T in the pump line, and the screen on its mount.

05

Wire it

Stock wiring integration keeps the factory harness and adds seven mains wires and six low-voltage ones: the SSR takes over the heater and the switches signal the PCB. 06

Check it, unplugged

About 63 checks with the cord out, picked for your schematic: earth reaches the metal, nothing shorts, the elements read what the rating plate predicts and every switch input is clean. It only says ready when all of them pass.

Power-on checks
07

First power-on

Covers on, plugged into a protected outlet. Flush with the portafilter out until water runs from the group, so the boiler fills before it heats. Then, with a blank basket, [UT] Test OPV should peak at 10.5 to 13 bar.

Runbook, phase 5Troubleshooting
08

Pull a profiled shot

Calibrate the pump, then let a profile run: a gentle preinfusion, a ramp to nine bar, and a taper as the puck gives way. The graph draws as the glass fills.
