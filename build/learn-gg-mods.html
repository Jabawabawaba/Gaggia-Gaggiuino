Hardware scales

under the drip tray, to J8

Two load cells on a printed base under the drip tray weigh the cup as the shot runs, so Gaggiuino can stop at a target weight and plot it live. Without them it still predicts the weight from the pump's flow; a Bluetooth scale is the other option.

How it works

A load cell is a metal bar with strain gauges glued to it, wired as a bridge. The weight of the cup bends the bar by a tiny amount and the bridge puts out a few millivolts at most. Each cell has its own HX711 channel, which amplifies and measures that very finely; the two share the clock line on J8 and each has its own data line, D1 and D2. The PCB adds the two, subtracts the tare and converts to grams after you calibrate with a known weight.

The official design is made for the Gaggia: 750 g cells in a printed base, with drip tray adapters for the Classic, the Pro (its tray's sides get trimmed) or a low-profile trayyour Classic's tray (with four spacers) or a low-profile trayyour Pro-family tray (its sides get trimmed) or a low-profile tray. Buy three or four cells; quality varies and they're easy to damage while preparing them.

Tank level sensor

above the tank, to J2

The ToFnLED board's distance sensor, mounted above the water, tells the screen how full the tank is, so you see it running low before the pump runs dry. It plugs into the Qwiic socket, J2, on the back of the PCB. The board also has an RGB LED that lights the tank.

How it works

A time-of-flight sensor fires a very short pulse of infrared light and times how long the reflection takes to come back from the water surface. Light covers a millimeter in about 3 trillionths of a second, so the chip, a VL53L0X in the Gen 2 code, has to time it extremely finely; it reports a distance, and the firmware turns the distance into a percentage. The light spreads in a cone, so the board needs to sit centered over the water, clear of the tubes; the official guide also shows how to join the intake and OPV tubes above the tank to keep them out of its view.

Shelly smart relay

an unofficial add-on, at the socket

A Shelly 1PM, a small Wi-Fi relay, sits between the socket and the machine and switches it on and off from your phone or a schedule. The power switch stays on the front, but it now only tells the Shelly what to do. Nothing about it is in the official docs.

How it works

The Shelly powers itself from L and N. Its relay connects L to O, and O feeds everything the power switch used to, so with the relay open the PCB, the SSR and the lamps get nothing. Its SW input sees mains while the power switch is on: in Switch mode the relay follows the switch, in Button mode each press toggles it, and the app can switch it either way. It also measures the power the machine draws. The PCB doesn't know it's there; it simply powers up when the relay closes.

The Shelly guide covers the part, where it goes and how to set it up.

Shelly smart relay

optional and unofficial

Some builders add a Shelly smart relay, so the machine can be switched on from a phone or on a schedule. It isn't in the official docs. Turn it on under Extras in Your Gaggia and its wiring, checks and steps are added to the pages; it's drawn for Pro-family machines on custom wiring.
