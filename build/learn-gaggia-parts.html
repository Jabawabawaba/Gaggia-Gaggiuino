1

Water tank

A plastic tank of about 2.1 liters, filled through the lid in the cup tray on top or slid out to fill at the sink. A tube from near its floor feeds the pump, and the over-pressure valve's return tube runs back into it.

How it works

Nothing clever: the pump sucks water up the intake tube, and the tank isn't pressurized, so anything the over-pressure valve dumps simply runs back in. Keep the end of the intake under water; a pump drawing air rattles and stops pushing water.

In a Gaggiuino build: unchanged. The optional ToFnLED sits above it, reads the level and lights the tank.

A coil yanks a piston against a spring once per mains cycle, and check valves turn that buzz into one-way flow.

How it works

Inside is an iron piston in a tube, with a coil wound around the outside and a spring behind it. A diode in the pump lets only one half of each mains cycle through the coil, so each cycle gives one pulse: the coil pulls the piston against the spring, then the spring pushes it back. Check valves only let water move toward the boiler, so every stroke shoves a little water forward. It's rated for 15 bar, well past brewing pressure, so something has to cap it: that's the over-pressure valve on top, on the group's inlet on your Classic. Over-pressure valve (OPV)

A spring-loaded valve between the pump and the boiler, bolted to the group's inlet on your Classic, on top of the pump on the Pro family. When the pressure passes its setting it opens and sends the extra water back to the tank, so the pressure can't climb any further.

How it works

A seal is held against a seat by a spring. Water pressure pushes the other way; when it beats the spring, the seal lifts and water escapes down the return tube. The more the pump pushes past the setting, the further it opens, so the line pressure stays close to the setting.

Boiler

A small aluminum boiler (lead-free brass on the E24), roughly 75 to 110 ml depending on the source,A small uncoated aluminum boiler, 75 to 105 ml depending on the source,A small aluminum boiler, about 105 ml, with a non-stick coating inside on the 2023 machines,A small lead-free brass boiler, just over 100 ml, nearly three times the weight of the aluminum one it replaced, sitting on the brass group body with an O-ring between them; four screws from under the frame hold the pair together. It heats the water for shots and makes the steam.

How it works

The pump pushes water in through an inlet connector on the group body; hot water leaves for the group through the 3-way valve, and a pipe from the top feeds the steam valve. The brew thermostat screws into its side and the steam thermostat into its top. It's small, so it heats quickly.

For brewing the brew thermostat holds it a little above 100 °C; the boiler is sealed, so the water stays liquid. For steam it's heated well past boiling, and the pressure that builds pushes steam out of the wand. The 2023 Evo's boiler has a non-stick coating, and the E24's is lead-free brass. On some 2023 Evos the coating flaked ("Boilergate"); Gaggia blamed how it was applied, and later Evos reportedly went back to uncoated aluminum. A used one is worth a look inside: dark flakes in the water mean a failing coating. Brass holds more heat than aluminum, so it swings less once hot, but takes longer to get there: about 90 seconds to brew-ready in Whole Latte Love's test, against about 70 for the Evo Pro.

In a Gaggiuino build: kept as it is. The thermocouple screws into the brew thermostat's M4 hole in its side.

Thermostats and thermal fuse

A 107 °C brew thermostat screwed into the side of the boiler, a 145 °C steam thermostat in its top, and a 184 °C one-shot thermal fuse. The thermostats switch the elements straight on and off.

How it works

Each thermostat holds a bimetal disc that snaps over at its set temperature and pushes a pair of contacts apart; as it cools a few degrees it snaps back and they close. That gap is why the boiler temperature swings. On the Pro family both sit on the elements' neutral side, in series,On your Classic both sit on the line side, ahead of the elements, in series, and the steam switch shorts out the brew thermostat so heating carries on to the steam one.

The fuse can't reset: once it melts, that path stays open until it's replaced. On the Pro it's in the switched line and feeds everything but the power lamp; on the original Classic it sits in the neutral feed to the whole machine, with the thermostats on the line side instead. It's in the switched line, after the power lamp, so it feeds everything else. On your Classic it sits in the neutral feed to the whole machine, so when it goes everything goes dark, the power lamp too.

In a Gaggiuino build: both thermostats leave the circuit: the thermocouple goes in the brew thermostat's hole and the SSR does the switching. The fuse stays as the safety cut-out.

7

3-way solenoid valve

An electrically worked valve between the boiler and the group. Powered, it lets boiler water through to the group. Unpowered, it shuts the boiler off and opens the group to a drain tube into the drip tray.

How it works

A coil pulls a plunger that seals one port or the other. In the stock circuit it gets power with the brew switch, and its return to neutral runs through the steam switch, so it can only open with steam off. That's what makes hot water mode work: brew and steam together run the pump with the valve shut.

The 230 V Classic sold from 2015 to 2018, the "V2", has no 3-way valve at all.

In a Gaggiuino build: kept. The PCB opens it from J13, and Gaggiuino depends on it for pressure release and flushing, which is why the V2 can't be converted.

A commercial-size 58 mm portafilter: chromed brass on the Classic and Classic Pro, stainless on the Evo and E24 in chrome-plated brass in stainless steel.

How it works

It locks into the group against a rubber gasket, 8.5 mm thick on the Classic and Classic Pro, 8.5 mm thick. The basket holds the coffee, and the spouts split the shot into one or two cups.

Steam valve and wand

A valve fed from the top of the boiler, opened by the knob on the right side, and the wand it feeds. The original Classic has a Pannarello frother; the Pro family has a commercial-style wand with a two-hole tip. A commercial-style stainless wand with a two-hole tip, which swings side to side but has no ball joint, and an insulated grip.

How it works

With the steam switch on, the boiler heats well past boiling and the knob lets the steam out. For hot water, the brew and steam switches go on together with the knob open: the pump pushes water through the boiler and out of the wand.

In a Gaggiuino build: kept. Hot water is still brew and steam together, and the steam temperature now comes from a setting instead of the steam thermostat.

Drip tray

Catches drips, and the water and pressure the 3-way valve vents after every shot.

How it works

The valve's drain tube points down into it, so the "whoosh" at the end of a shot lands here.

In a Gaggiuino build: unchanged. The optional hardware scales go under it.
