The circuit

switch it yourself

Every load in the stock Classic ProEvo Pro, wired like the Classic Pro,E24, wired like the Classic Pro,Classic sits on its own path from line to neutral, and every path runs through a switch or a thermostat. Nothing is computed: if a path is closed end to end, that part runs. The diagram is drawn as a ladder, with line down the left, neutral down the right, and one rung per load.

Three details surprise people. The thermostats sit on the elements' neutral side, after them. The brew and steam lamps aren't heating lamps: each is wired across its thermostat, so it lights when that thermostat opens, which means ready. And the 184 °C thermal fuse feeds everything except the power lamp, so a blown fuse leaves the power lamp lit and nothing else working.

Three details surprise people. The power switch breaks the line only, and the thermostats sit on the line side, ahead of the elements. There's one ready lamp, not a heating lamp: it's wired across both thermostats, so it lights whenever heating stops, for brewing or steam. And the 184 °C thermal fuse is in the neutral feed to the whole machine, so a blown fuse leaves everything dark, the power lamp too.

Try it

  • Turn on Power and let it run: the elements heat until the brew thermostat opens, and the brewready lamp lights each time it does.
  • Turn on Brew: the pump runs, and the 3-way valve opens because its returnfeed through the steam switch is closed.
  • Add Steam: that's hot water mode. The pump keeps running, the valve shuts, and the heater carries on to the steam thermostat.
  • Steam alone: it shorts out the brew thermostat, so heating carries on until the steam thermostat opens at %%STEAM_OPEN%% °C and the steamready lamp lights.
  • Drag the temperature past %%FUSE%% °C: the fuse melts and only the power lamp stays liteverything goes dark until a new one is fitted.

With Gaggiuino, the brew and steam switches stop switching these rungs and signal the PCB at low voltage instead, and both thermostats leave the elements' circuit: the thermocouple and the SSR take over. With custom wiring that's all the two switches carry; with stock wiring integration, some of their other spades stay on mains. The power switch, the thermal fuse, the pump's protector and the earth stay. See what changes.

A stock shot, start to finish

  1. Switch on. The elements heat with the brewready lamp out, and the small boiler warms the brass group under it.
  2. The brew thermostat opens and the brewready lamp lights, but heat already in the elements keeps the water rising for a while: overshoot.
  3. It cools several degrees, the thermostat closes, the lamp goes out, and the cycle repeats for as long as the machine is on. The water temperature at any moment depends on where you are in that cycle.
  4. Owners temperature-surf: flush some water, then time the shot from a lamp change, to land at the same point in the swing every time.
  5. Brew switch on. The pump and the 3-way valve run; pressure builds against the puck, up to the over-pressure valve's setting at most.
  6. Brew switch off. The valve vents the group to the drip tray, so the puck is left drier and the portafilter comes off without spraying.
  7. For milk, switch on steam and wait for the steamready lamp, purge the wand, then steam. Afterward, bring the boiler back down by running water through the group, with no portafilter, before the next shot.

Gaggiuino replaces steps 2 to 4 with a temperature that holds still, and step 5 with a pressure or flow profile.
