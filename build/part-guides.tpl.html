Thermocouple

K type · M4 × 6 mm

You need
  • The thermocouple: K type, ungrounded, M4 thread
  • Thermal paste rated for 180 °C or more
  • A spanner for the old thermostat
  • Hex keys for the boiler's four screws, if you tip it up
  • A multimeter
What it does

It reports the boiler's temperature to the PCB on J4. Ungrounded means its junction is insulated from the probe body, because the low-voltage side floats; How Gaggiuino works explains why.

    Pressure sensor

    1.2 MPa · 0 to 12 bar

    You need
    • The sensor and its O-rings
    • The tap block with its small push-in fitting
    • The sensor's push-in fitting (1/4" thread to 6 mm tube)
    • 40 mm of 4 × 6 mm PTFE tube and a sharp cutter
    • Two longer M5 screws (25 mm on the official parts list)
    • A towel
    • The sensor and its flat O-ring
    • Classic Pro family: a 6 mm barbed T, a 6 mm barb to 1/4" sensor fitting, 200 to 250 mm of braided hose, four hose clamps and clamp pliers
    • Original Classic: a 6 mm push-to-connect T, a push-in sensor fitting (1/4" to 6 mm), 200 to 250 mm of 4 × 6 mm PTFE tube
    • A sharp cutter
    What it does

    It tells the PCB the brew pressure, 0 to 12 bar, on J6, so Gaggiuino can profile pressure. It seals on an O-ring, not on its thread.

    Two O-ring jobs. A flat white one, about 11 mm across and 3 mm thick, seals the sensor in its fitting. A small round silicone one, 6 mm inside and 1.8 mm thick, seals the block against the group. Check your kit has one of each: the block's is a size 010 O-ring.

      Resettable fuse

      185 °C · 250 V 16 A · optional

      Optional. The official wiring basics page says a kit's resettable fuse goes in the steam thermostat's slot as a replacement for the stock 184 °C one-shot fuse, on any install. The stock wiring guide itself keeps the stock fuse and gives no steps for the swap, so these follow the custom wiring guide: the old fuse's wires move onto the new one. If you'd rather follow the stock guide exactly, keep it as a spare.

      On stock wiring it carries everything the machine draws: about 12 A at 120 V and 6 A at 230 V, inside its 16 A rating.

      Optional. The custom wiring guide lists it as a substitute for the stock one-shot fuse, in the steam thermostat's hole. In custom wiring it sits in the heater line.

      You need
      • The resettable fuse: 185 °C, 16 A, M4 stud
      • The same 180 °C thermal paste
      • Uninsulated 6.3 mm female spades, silicone sleeve and an open-barrel crimper
      • 18 AWG silicone wire, a bare BN1.25 butt splice and silicone heat shrink, if a wire won't reach
      • A multimeter
      What it does

      Like the thermostats it's a bimetal switch, but it opens only at 185 °C, as a last resort if the boiler overheats, and stays open until you press its button. The stock fuse does the same job once and then has to be replaced.

        Making the wires

        strip · crimp · insulate · pull-test

        You need
        • 18 AWG silicone wire for mains, 22 AWG for low voltage, cut from the cut list
        • A wire stripper
        • A ratcheting crimper for insulated terminals (red, blue, yellow dies)
        • An open-barrel crimper for the bare spades at the boiler, and an uninsulated-terminal crimper for any BN splices
        • 6.3 mm spades (insulated, bare and piggyback), forks, BN1.25 splices
        • Silicone heat shrink or self-fusing silicone tape, and a heat gun
        Which crimper is which

        An insulated-terminal crimper has colored, rounded dies and squashes a nylon sleeve. An open-barrel crimper has small B-shaped dies that roll a bare terminal's tabs over the wire. A third kind, with a punch and block, crimps bare BN splices. Most hardware-store crimpers are the insulated kind; bare open-barrel spades at the boiler need the open-barrel kind.

          Solid-state relay

          SSR-40DA · back wall

          You need
          • The SSR (SSR-40DA)
          • An M4 × 10 screw, washer and nut, or an 8-32 set
          • Forks for all four screws: SV1.25-5 for one wire, SV2-5 for two, as on the official parts list
          • A screwdriver for the terminal screws
          What it does

          It switches the heater on the board's command, with the board's 5 V side isolated from mains. How Gaggiuino works explains it.

            Switches

            stock wiring

            On custom wiring the brew and steam switches carry only the low-voltage wires your harness drawing shows. The moves below are for stock wiring.

            You need
            • Your printed wire labels
            • Silicone tape or silicone heat shrink for the connectors that stay live
            • Cable ties
            • The low-voltage switch wires, with 6.3 mm female spades (the ground and its jumper share one)
            What changes

            The switches also become signals for the PCB: one pole of each, when on, joins a PCB input to the 5 V ground. The other spades still carry mains, so the low-voltage wires go only on the ones this frees, and the moved connectors stay live.

              The screen, accessories and firmware

              Not wiring, but part of the build. The official guides have the full detail; each step here links to its photo.

              Screen housing

              Gen 3, 4.3"

              You need
              • The screen and its cable, already tested on the bench
              • The printed housing (back, front cover) and the mount you chose: front, funnel or rear
              • Six 20 × 10 × 3 mm rectangular magnets and super glue or epoxy (80 °C double-sided tape on a machine that isn't magnetic)
              • The adjustable plate
              • Three M3 × 30 mm socket head screws, five M3 washers (7 mm OD or less), five split lock washers and two M3 nylock nuts
              • The funnel cover, its latch, an M3 heat-set insert (4.5 or 4.6 mm OD, 5 or 6 mm long) and a soldering iron
              • Two M3 × 30 mm socket head screws, four M3 washers (7 mm OD or less), four split lock washers and two M3 nylock nuts
              • Kapton tape (20 mm) or other high-temperature tape
              What it does

              It holds the screen where you can reach it and keeps its cable out of the way. Front: magnets on the machine's front. Funnel: on the funnel, swinging aside to fill the tank. Rear: hooked into the back vent.

                ToFnLED

                optional · tank level and light

                You need
                • The ToFnLED board (NCP5623 or PCA9632 LED controller)
                • A Qwiic cable (JST-SH, 4-pin), 250 mm or more
                • The printed housing, two 5 × 1 mm disc magnets and super glue (or 80 °C double-sided tape)
                • Optional: a 6 mm barbed T to join the tank tubes
                What it does

                A time-of-flight sensor measures the distance down to the water, so the screen shows the tank level, and an RGB LED lights the tank. It talks to the PCB over I2C on J2.

                  Firmware updates

                  Gen 3 · PCB v4

                  PCB v3.x board repair. Some PCB v3.1, v3 and v2 boards can switch the heaters on while a debugger is connected. The official PCB upgrade page has a test and a resistor fix; without it, flash those boards with the machine unplugged. PCB v4 doesn't need it. PA15 pull-down rework

                  You need
                  • A phone or computer on the machine's Wi-Fi
                  • The three files from the official release
                  • A screen or headless board enabled for Gen 3 by an official supplier, as the official flashing page requires
                  • A computer with STM32CubeProgrammer
                  • A WeAct Mini Debugger or an ST-Link, with four jumper wires
                  What it does

                  Updates the core (the STM32 on the PCB) and the screen (its ESP32 and the web UI). On PCB v4 all three go over Wi-Fi; the wire is the fallback for the core, and the only way on PCB v3.1.

                    Hardware scales

                    optional · weight in the cup

                    You need
                    • The printed scales base, center cover and the tray adapter for your tray
                    • Two 750 g load cells (buy three or four: quality varies)
                    • Two HX711 boards and a 1 kΩ resistor, or a dualScaleBoard, or the FPC dualScaleBoard with its cable adapter board and a 600 mm FPC cable (0.5 mm, 10P, forward direction)
                    • M3 flat-head screws (two magnetic), two self-tapping screws, twenty 5 × 2 mm magnets, gap-filling super glue
                    • Stripboard, five right-angle header pins, 26 AWG wire, a 5-pin JST-XH and a 5-pin JST-PH (FPC board: no stripboard, a JST-XH to PH lead of 150 mm or more, and 80 °C double-sided tape)
                    • A 200 to 400 g object and an accurate scale, for calibration
                    What it does

                    Weighs the shot as it runs, through J8. Without them Gaggiuino estimates the weight (predictive scales), which is enough for most; the official guide calls this an advanced build that needs accurate prints.

                      Shelly smart relay

                      add-on · unofficial · Pro family, custom wiring

                      Unofficial, and always live. Nothing about the Shelly is in the Gaggiuino docs: this follows fusio's Classic Pro and Shelly Plus 1PM build, shared on the Gaggiuino Discord, changed so the machine's neutral never runs through the Shelly. The socket, the Shelly, the power switch and their wires are live whenever the cord is in, even with the machine off, and the relay breaks line only. Unplug before you open anything.

                      You need
                      • A Shelly 1PM Gen4, the UL-certified version in North America
                      • An enclosure for it and the socket: fusio's socket-only print on MakerWorld, ordered from a print service if you have no printer
                      • SH1 to SH4 from the cut list, a two-wire spade, a piggyback spade and a BN2 splice
                      • Four 0.75 mm² (18 AWG) insulated ferrules with 6 mm sleeves, and a ferrule crimper
                      • A grommet or sleeve for the socket hole
                      What it does

                      Switches the whole machine on and off from the Shelly app, a schedule or the power switch, which now only signals it. It also reports the power the machine draws. The PCB doesn't know it's there: it simply powers up when the Shelly switches on.
